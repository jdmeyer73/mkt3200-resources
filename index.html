Summit Trail & Sport

Outdoor & Sporting Goods Retailer

Business Overview

Summit Trail & Sport is a locally owned outdoor and sporting goods retailer that has served northwest Ohio for approximately 18 years. The company operates two stores: one in a suburban retail area and another in a smaller college-oriented community. Customers can also shop through the company’s e-commerce website and choose home delivery or in-store pickup.

The company serves a broad mix of customers, ranging from serious outdoor and sports enthusiasts to families and more casual recreational consumers.

Products

  • Camping and hiking equipment
  • Outdoor apparel and footwear
  • Running and fitness products
  • Team and recreational sporting goods
  • Fishing equipment
  • Seasonal outdoor products and accessories

Competitive Environment

Summit Trail & Sport competes with national sporting-goods chains, outdoor specialty retailers, mass merchants, Amazon and other online sellers, and manufacturers that sell directly to consumers.

How the Company Competes

Rather than trying to be the lowest-price retailer, Summit Trail & Sport emphasizes product selection, knowledgeable employees, customer service, and its connection to the local outdoor and sports community.

Master Research Objectives
Summit Trail & Sport
Synthesized from Project Groups 11–18
Management Decision Problem
What changes should Summit Trail & Sport make to its marketing, in-store experience, and online retail strategy to more effectively attract, convert, and retain younger and casual outdoor/sporting-goods shoppers while maintaining the strengths valued by its established customers?
Master Research Objectives

The objectives below represent a synthesis from the group interviews and research requests. They are broad enough to support multiple questionnaire measures while still focusing on information management can use in making decisions.

Research Objective 1: Retailer Choice Factors

Determine the factors that most influence younger and casual shoppers when choosing Summit Trail & Sport versus competing outdoor and sporting-goods retailers.

Information the Objective Should Provide: Identify the relative importance of factors such as price/value, product selection, convenience, brand familiarity, staff expertise, store experience, and online options. This information will help management understand where Summit is winning or losing customers at the purchase-decision stage and where limited resources could have the greatest effect.
Research Objective 2: Customer Experience and Retention

Assess how younger and casual shoppers perceive Summit Trail & Sport's in-store experience and identify the factors that encourage or discourage repeat visits and purchases.

Information the Objective Should Provide: Evaluate satisfaction and perceived value along with store layout, ease of finding products, product assortment, staff approachability and expertise, convenience, and whether the store feels welcoming or overly specialized to newer participants. Compare these experiences with customers' intentions to return and shop Summit again. This information will help management identify which aspects of the customer experience are most important for building stronger relationships and repeat purchasing among younger and casual customers.
Research Objective 3: Online Shopping and Conversion

Determine why website traffic and online product interest are not translating into online purchases at the expected rate.

Information the Objective Should Provide: Identify how customers use the website, where barriers or drop-off may occur, and the roles of usability, inventory information, pricing, fulfillment options, product research, and comparison shopping. This information will help management decide which digital-shopping improvements are most likely to increase conversion rather than simply increase traffic.
Research Objective 4: Awareness and Marketing Effectiveness

Evaluate how effectively Summit Trail & Sport's marketing reaches younger and casual shoppers and which channels and messages are most likely to generate meaningful engagement and purchasing.

Information the Objective Should Provide: Measure awareness of the brand, stores, website, and product offerings; identify the channels through which customers encounter Summit; and assess which types of messages or promotions influence store visits and purchases. This information will help management allocate marketing resources more effectively.
Use in Group Task 2
These four master objectives replace the individual group objectives from Group Task 1 for purposes of questionnaire development. In the Research Objectives Auction, each of the eight teams will ultimately be assigned two different objectives, allowing each master objective to be covered by four teams.

Teams should develop measures and questionnaire items that directly address their assigned master objectives, while recognizing that some measures may contribute to more than one objective.
