River Rock Coffee

Independent Coffee Shop & Café

Business Overview

River Rock Coffee is a locally owned independent coffee shop and café serving a northwest Ohio community. The business offers specialty coffee and espresso drinks along with food and bakery items in a comfortable setting designed for customers who want to meet, work, study, or simply enjoy a drink or meal.

The café serves a broad mix of customers, including college students, working professionals, local residents, families, and both frequent and occasional coffee-shop visitors.

Menu

  • Coffee and espresso drinks
  • Cold coffee and specialty beverages
  • Tea and non-coffee beverages
  • Pastries and baked goods
  • Breakfast and light meal options
  • Seasonal food and beverage offerings

Competitive Environment

River Rock Coffee competes with national coffee chains, other independent coffee shops, fast-food restaurants offering coffee and breakfast items, convenience stores, and customers’ ability to prepare coffee and other beverages at home.

How the Company Competes

Rather than competing primarily on price, River Rock Coffee emphasizes beverage quality, personalized service, a welcoming café environment, locally oriented community connections, and an experience that gives customers a reason to choose an independent coffee shop.

Your Role

If your class selects River Rock Coffee, your team will interview the business owner to learn more about the company’s current situation, the decisions facing management, and the information the owner needs before moving forward.

Master Research Objectives
River Rock Coffee
Synthesized from Project Groups 21–29
Management Decision Problem
What changes should River Rock Coffee make to generate more consistent customer traffic and spending beyond the morning rush while remaining competitive with convenience-oriented chains and preserving the quality, atmosphere, and local character that distinguish the business?
Master Research Objectives

The objectives below represent a synthesis from the group interviews and research requests. They are broad enough to support multiple questionnaire measures while remaining focused on information management can use in making decisions.

Research Objective 1: Afternoon and Early-Evening Demand

Identify the factors that influence customers' likelihood of visiting River Rock Coffee during the afternoon and early-evening periods.

Information the Objective Should Provide: Determine why customers are less likely to visit after the morning rush and identify barriers and motivators related to daypart needs, schedules, menu relevance, promotions, atmosphere, convenience, and other alternatives. This information will help management identify practical ways to create more consistent traffic during slower periods.
Research Objective 2: Competitive Choice and Convenience

Determine the factors that influence customers' choice between River Rock Coffee and competing coffee shops or other convenience-based alternatives.

Information the Objective Should Provide: Measure the importance of speed, ordering convenience, parking/access, price/value, product quality, atmosphere, service, loyalty benefits, and local identity. This information will help management understand which competitive disadvantages matter most and which convenience improvements could be made without undermining River Rock's positioning.
Research Objective 3: Menu and Product Opportunities

Evaluate customer preferences and unmet needs for food and beverage offerings that could broaden River Rock Coffee's appeal, particularly during slower dayparts.

Information the Objective Should Provide: Assess awareness and perceptions of the current menu, interest in more substantial food, grab-and-go items, seasonal or specialty beverages, and other offerings that could make River Rock relevant for additional occasions. This information will help management identify product opportunities that could attract visits beyond the morning coffee occasion.
Research Objective 4: Pricing, Promotions, and Customer Spending

Determine how pricing, bundles, promotions, and other purchase incentives could influence customer spending and visit behavior.

Information the Objective Should Provide: Measure perceptions of price and value, willingness to purchase bundles or add-ons, responsiveness to daypart-specific promotions, and the types of incentives most likely to increase basket size or encourage visits during slower periods. This information will help management evaluate revenue-building tactics without relying solely on higher customer traffic.
Research Objective 5: Digital Ordering and Loyalty

Assess how customers use and value River Rock Coffee's online ordering and loyalty program and which improvements would be most likely to influence visit frequency or purchasing behavior.

Information the Objective Should Provide: Determine current usage, satisfaction, and barriers associated with digital ordering and loyalty features, including whether greater integration or off-peak rewards would change behavior. This information will help management distinguish features that merely reward existing regulars from those that could generate incremental visits or purchases.
Research Objective 6: Brand Perceptions and Marketing Effectiveness

Measure how current and potential customers perceive River Rock Coffee and evaluate how effectively its marketing communicates the attributes that could motivate trial, repeat visits, and off-peak use.

Information the Objective Should Provide: Assess awareness and perceptions of River Rock's quality, atmosphere, service, local/community orientation, menu breadth, and overall value, along with the channels and messages that reach customers. This information will help management strengthen brand positioning and attract customers beyond its existing core of regulars.
Use in Group Task 2
These six master objectives replace the individual group objectives from Group Task 1 for purposes of questionnaire development. In the Research Objectives Auction, each of the nine teams will ultimately be assigned two different objectives, allowing each master objective to be covered by three teams.

Teams should develop measures and questionnaire items that directly address their assigned master objectives, while recognizing that some measures may contribute to more than one objective.
